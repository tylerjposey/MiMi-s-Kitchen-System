---
name: Historical provenance
description: Rules for honest kitchen-cost history when earlier events were not recorded.
---

Treat historical points as observations, not estimates. Older records created before tracking began have no known series; show an empty history until real changes are captured. On the first edit, preserve the previously observed state before recording the new one, but do not pretend earlier menu profitability was measured on past dates.

**Why:** Current prices and recipes cannot reconstruct what they cost at arbitrary earlier times. A fabricated chart or alert would mislead the owner. Purchase units can also change, making a line between raw costs per pound and per ounce mathematically misleading.

**How to apply:** Base alerts on consecutive actual records and label missing history clearly. Normalize compatible units to a common base before comparing or charting costs; do not compare incompatible measurement types. Keep ordinary dollar amounts at cents precision, but allow finer precision for cost per unit when two decimals would incorrectly display a nonzero cost as $0.00.
